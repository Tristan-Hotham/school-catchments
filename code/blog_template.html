There is no map of school catchments for the UK. So I made the nearest thing: every one of the UK's {{schools.all_n|int}} state schools, the official catchments of the {{catchments.authorities|int}} councils that publish them openly, and the patch of land nearest to each school everywhere else.

"Are we in the catchment?" is one of the first questions any parent asks about a school. The answer depends enormously on where you live. In a city a primary school might serve a few hundred metres of streets. In the far north of Scotland a single high school can be responsible for a slice of moor, mountain and coast bigger than an English county. I wanted to see that whole range on one map.

The trouble is that nobody publishes a UK catchment map. Scotland's councils draw a catchment for almost every school, but England, Wales and Northern Ireland have no national set. Many English councils have no catchments at all, and Northern Ireland admits pupils by each school's own criteria. So my map does two things. Where a council publishes its catchments as open data, I show them: {{catchments.n|int}} areas from {{catchments.authorities|int}} councils, {{catchments.by_nation.Scotland.authorities|int}} of them Scottish. Everywhere else I draw nearest-school zones: the land that is closer, as the crow flies, to one school than to any other. They are a yardstick, not catchments, and the map marks them differently.

From a few streets to a county

How far is it to school?

I measured the straight-line distance from each of Great Britain's {{settlements.villages_hamlets|int}} villages and hamlets in Ordnance Survey's gazetteer to the nearest state school. Half of English villages and hamlets are within {{settlements.by_nation.England.prim_median|f1}} km of a primary. In Scotland {{settlements.by_nation.Scotland.prim_gt5|pct}} are more than 5 km from one, and {{settlements.by_nation.Scotland.sec_gt10|pct}} are more than 10 km from a secondary school.

The most remote places are islands, where a straight line across the sea says little about the real journey. {{settlements.farthest_secondary.0.name|raw}}, in {{d.far_county|raw}}, is {{settlements.farthest_secondary.0.km|f1}} km from the nearest secondary school. On the mainland the furthest is {{settlements.farthest_secondary_mainland.0.name|raw}} in {{settlements.farthest_secondary_mainland.0.county|raw}}, {{settlements.farthest_secondary_mainland.0.km|f1}} km from a secondary.

Is the nearest school your catchment school?

Where councils publish catchments I could test my yardstick. For every official catchment I laid a grid of points over it and asked whether the catchment's school was also the nearest school of its kind. Weighted by area, it was for {{agreement.all|pct}} of the land. So nearest-school zones are a fair first guess, but about a third of the time the official answer is a different school, because catchments follow roads, rivers, villages and the size of buildings rather than straight lines.

How I did this

The schools come from the four governments' open lists: Get Information About Schools for England, the Welsh Government's address list, the Scottish Government's school roll and locations, and the Department of Education's enrolment data for Northern Ireland, which I placed by postcode. The catchments come from councils' open map services found through data.gov.uk and ArcGIS Online. Scotland also has a national catchment layer on the Improvement Service's Spatial Hub, but it sits behind a terms form, so I used the councils' own open services instead. Nearest-school zones are Voronoi cells clipped to land. Everything is measured in straight lines, which ignore roads and ferries, and the zones ignore faith, selection and how full a school is. This is a map for curiosity, not for applications: always check with your council.
