School catchments

How much of the UK does one school serve? Every open state school, the official catchments that councils publish, and the nearest school everywhere else.

Dr Tristan Hotham · Independent researcher · blog.tristanhotham.com

Ask a parent in Hackney and a parent in Sutherland how far it is to school and you will get very different answers. In a city a primary school might serve a few hundred metres of streets. In the far north of Scotland a single high school can be responsible for a stretch of moor, mountain and coast bigger than some English counties. I wanted to see that difference on one map: every school in the UK, and the patch of land each one serves.

That turned out to be harder than it sounds, because there is no UK map of school catchments. Scotland's councils draw a catchment for almost every school and many publish them, but England, Wales and Northern Ireland have no national set: English admissions mostly work on distance and oversubscription rules, and only some councils publish the areas they use. So this map does two things. Where a council publishes official catchments openly, I show them, 2,992 areas from 33 councils. Everywhere else I draw nearest-school zones: the land that is closer, as the crow flies, to one school than to any other. These are a geometric yardstick, not catchments, and the map marks them differently.

Not an admissions tool. Nearest-school zones are not catchments. Real admissions depend on each school's rules, faith criteria, siblings, walking routes and how full the school is. Distances here are straight lines, not journeys, and islands make that especially rough. For a real application, always check with your council.

    02 · THE BIGGEST CATCHMENTSHighland high schools the size of counties

    Of the official catchments I could gather, the biggest are all in the Highlands and Islands. Golspie High School's catchment covers 2,215 km² of land, for a school of 264 pupils; Lochaber High School serves 2,048 km² and Ullapool High School 2,031 km². For scale, the whole of Herefordshire is about 2,180 km². The biggest primary catchment, Ullapool Primary School, covers 986 km². England's biggest official catchment in this set is Haydon Bridge High School in Northumberland, at 1,663 km².

    Where there is no published catchment, the nearest-school zones tell the same story. The biggest primary zone in the UK belongs to Tongue Primary School (841 km², 27 pupils), and the biggest secondary zone to Ullapool High School (2,390 km²). In England the biggest primary zone is Kielder Primary School and Nursery's, 298 km² of Northumberland forest and fell for 10 pupils; in Wales, Ysgol Dolafon in Powys (345 km²); in Northern Ireland, St Caireall's Primary School, Castlederg (145 km²).

    03 · THE BIGGEST SCHOOLSA small Highland school and a London giant

    Area and size run in opposite directions. The schools with the most land usually have the fewest pupils: half of Scotland's primaries have 176 pupils or fewer, against 225 in England, and 176 Scottish primaries have fewer than 25. At the other end, the biggest state secondary in the UK is Brampton Manor Academy in Newham with 2,987 pupils, and the biggest primary is Claycots School in Slough with 1,605. The biggest in the other nations: Holyrood Secondary School (2,269) in Scotland, Whitchurch High School (2,459) in Wales and Bangor Academy & Sixth Form College (1,867) in Northern Ireland.

    04 · HOW FAR TO SCHOOL?Rural distance to the nearest school

    I measured the straight-line distance from every village and hamlet in Great Britain's OS Open Names gazetteer, 28,055 places, to the nearest state primary and secondary school. In England half of them are within 1.6 km of a primary; in Scotland the median is 2.2 km, but 17% of Scottish villages and hamlets (576 places) are more than 5 km from one, against 2% in England and 7% in Wales. For secondary school the gap widens: 34% of Scottish villages and hamlets are more than 10 km from one.

    Villages and hamlets by distance to the nearest school
    Share of OS Open Names villages and hamlets in each straight-line distance band

    The most remote places are islands (3% of the villages and hamlets are off the mainland), where a straight line to the nearest school crosses the sea and the real journey involves a ferry or a stay in a hostel during the week. On this measure the furthest from a secondary school is Da Hametoon in Shetland Islands (42.2 km), and on the mainland Braemar in Aberdeenshire (37.5 km). Measured over land rather than settlements, 23% of Scotland is more than 10 km from a primary school; in England it is 0.4%.

    05 · CATCHMENT OR NEAREST?Two thirds of the time, the nearest school is your catchment school

    Where a council publishes catchments, I can test the yardstick. For every official catchment linked to its school, I laid a fine grid of points over it and asked: is this catchment's school also the nearest school of its kind? Weighted by area, the answer is yes for 66% of catchment land (66% for primary, 66% for secondary). So the nearest-school zones on the map are a fair first guess, but a third of the time the official answer is a different school, because catchments follow roads, rivers, parishes, history and the size of buildings rather than straight lines. In 22% of catchments, less than half of the land is nearest to the catchment's own school.

    How often the nearest school is the catchment school, by council

    Catholic catchments in Scotland are much bigger, because there are fewer Catholic schools: they agree with the nearest Catholic school for 74% of their land. Switch on "Catholic catchments instead" on the map to see them.

    06 · WHO PUBLISHES CATCHMENTS?Coverage, honestly

    I found official catchments published as open map services by 21 of Scotland's 32 councils, 11 English councils and 1 Welsh council. Scotland's councils also share a national catchment layer through the Improvement Service's Spatial Hub, but downloading it requires accepting the Hub's terms in a web form, so I used each council's own open service instead and left the rest as nearest-school zones. Northern Ireland has no catchments at all: admission is by each school's criteria. Many English councils have no catchments either, or publish them only as PDFs or postcode lookups.

    08 · METHODS AND CAVEATSWhat I did, and what to doubt

    • Schools. England: Get Information About Schools (GIAS), download of 28 October 2026, open establishments in the local authority maintained, academy, free school and special school groups; nurseries, sixth-form-only colleges and independent schools are left out; locations from GIAS eastings and northings; rolls from the January 2026 census as published in GIAS. Wales: the Welsh Government's address list of maintained schools from DataMapWales, with pupil numbers. Scotland: Scottish School Roll and Locations (Scottish Government), local authority schools. Northern Ireland: Department of Education school-level enrolment data for 2025/26; I located schools at their postcode centroid using postcodes.io (ONS Postcode Directory), and 2 schools whose postcodes were not found are left off. Primary includes middle schools deemed primary; secondary includes middle schools deemed secondary; all-through schools count as both. Scotland lists the departments of its combined schools (for example the primary and secondary of a junior high school) separately, and I count them separately too, so the 27,024 schools are 26,991 distinct establishments. I kept school names and published rolls only; no personal data.
    • Official catchments. I searched data.gov.uk and ArcGIS Online for council catchment services that open without a login, downloaded the polygons in the browser (simplified on the server to about 20 m), and chose one layer per council, phase and denomination by hand, preferring each council's own open-data account and the current year. I dropped polygons that are not a school's catchment (for example "no catchment area" or county-wide areas), clipped everything to the council's land using ONS boundaries, and linked each catchment to a school: a school of the same phase inside it, choosing the closest name if there are several (98% linked). Shared or choice areas and a few dated layers remain; Wokingham, Lancashire and Bristol publish overlapping priority areas that do not fit a one-school-one-area map, so I left them out.
    • Nearest-school zones. For each nation and phase, a Voronoi cell around every mainstream state school (special schools excluded), clipped to the nation's land, measured in British National Grid (Irish Transverse Mercator for Northern Ireland). Faith and non-faith schools are mixed together; zones stop at national borders; straight lines ignore roads, ferries and mountains. On the map the zones are recalculated in your browser for the schools on screen, which can differ by a few metres from my figures.
    • Agreement. Points on a grid of at least 200 m inside each linked catchment; "agrees" means the nearest school of the same phase (Catholic schools for denominational catchments; in Scotland non-Catholic schools for the others) is the catchment's school. The share is weighted by catchment area.
    • Distances. Straight lines from OS Open Names villages and hamlets (Great Britain only), and from a 1 km grid over all UK land, to the nearest mainstream state school of each phase in the same island (Great Britain or Ireland).
    • Honest limits. Council layers differ in date, care and purpose, and a few are labelled as working copies. Rolls are a single census day. A place's nearest school may be full, of the wrong faith or selective. This is a map for curiosity and comparison, not for applications.
